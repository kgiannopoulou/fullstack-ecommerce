## Why each decision was made

**1. Duplicate transaction records** (2,435 records, removed (kept first copy))

The integration re-sent records with the same transaction ID and identical content. Keeping them would double-count revenue and units.

**2. Negative quantity (legacy return adjustments)** (134 records, investigated, excluded (refund already on the order))

Every negative line mirrors a positive sale of the same product in the same order (see Q01.7). They are manual stock-return entries from the old POS; the money side of returns is already recorded as refund_amount, so counting them would deduct twice.

**3. Invalid unit price (zero, negative or >5x list)** (44 records, excluded)

Zero/negative prices and prices ~100x the list price (entered in cents) cannot be trusted. Too few to matter for totals (<0.02% of records), so they are excluded rather than guessed.

**4. Missing customer ID (guest checkout)** (5,469 records, retained as guest; excluded from customer metrics)

These are real, paid orders placed without an account. Dropping them would understate revenue; they are kept for sales KPIs (2,316 orders) but cannot be part of retention, RFM or cohort analysis.

**5. Missing category** (689 records, mapped from product master)

Category is an attribute of the product, not the sale. The product master has it for every SKU, so the value is recovered exactly instead of being bucketed as 'Unknown'.

**6. Missing payment method** (864 records, classified as unknown)

Payment type is not needed for revenue, so the orders stay; reporting them as 'Unknown' keeps the payment-mix percentages honest instead of silently redistributing them.

**7. Non-standard country name** (40,499 records, standardised to english country name)

'GR', 'Hellas', 'Ελλάδα' and 'greece' are one market. Without mapping, Greece would be split across six rows of every country report.

**8. Non-ISO date format** (115,031 records, parsed with explicit format rules)

Four formats are in use. Each is parsed with its own explicit rule (never guessed), so 05/03 is always 5 March. Where an order's lines disagree, the timestamp with a time of day is used.

**9. Non-standard order status spelling** (23,574 records, normalised)

'COMPLETE', 'Completed' and 'CANCELED' are the same states written by different systems.

**10. Cancelled order lines** (18,401 records, kept for cancellation kpis, excluded from revenue)

Cancelled orders were never shipped or paid, so they are not revenue - but they are an operational signal (cancellation rate by payment method), so the rows are kept.

**11. Refunded order lines** (12,748 records, kept; refund deducted in net revenue)

The goods were shipped; the refunded amount (full or partial) is subtracted in Net Revenue.

**12. Quantity outliers (>= 40 units)** (41 records, retained (verified bulk orders), flagged is_bulk)

Far above the P99 quantity, but prices and customers are normal: these are small businesses buying in bulk. They are real revenue, so they are kept and flagged so they can be excluded from per-order averages where they would distort them.

**13. Missing shipping cost (no matched courier invoice)** (2,862 records, imputed: median cost for the same courier and country)

The parcel was shipped (it has a courier and a delivery date), so the cost is real but the invoice line was not matched. Treating it as 0 would overstate gross profit; the courier-and-country median is a close estimate (1,205 orders, flagged shipping_cost_imputed).

**14. Duplicate inventory snapshots** (513 records, removed)

The warehouse system uploaded some days twice. One stock count per product per day is kept.

**15. Negative stock on hand** (378 records, set to 0, flagged stock_corrected)

Physical stock cannot be negative: these are counting errors on days the SKU had sold out. Treating them as 0 (out of stock) matches the sales recorded on those days.

**16. Non-standard country name (customer master)** (11,026 records, standardised to english country name)

Same mapping as the transaction table.

## What the cleaning does *not* do

- It does not delete cancelled orders: the cancellation rate is a KPI. Revenue queries filter them.
- It does not impute missing customers: guest revenue is real, but guests cannot have a retention curve.
- It does not cap outliers: bulk orders are genuine. Per-order averages are reported with and without them.

# Statistical tests

Generated by `analytics/stats_analysis.py`. Each row checks one claim from
[findings.md](findings.md) against the possibility that it is noise (alpha = 0.05).

| Claim | Test | Result | Statistic | p-value | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Repeat purchasing rose after the loyalty launch | Two-proportion z-test, 90-day repeat rate of first-time buyers | 27.8% -> 40.7% (+12.9 pp, 95% CI 12.2 to 13.7 pp) | z = 35.4 | < 0.001 | 72,575 |
| The loyalty effect holds with delivery held constant (on-time first orders only) | Two-proportion z-test | 33.5% -> 45.5% (+12.0 pp) | z = 23.3 | < 0.001 | 37,398 |
| A late first delivery lowers the chance of a second order | Chi-square test of independence (2x2) and odds ratio | repeat 38.2% on time vs 25.9% late; on-time buyers have 1.77x the odds of returning | chi2 = 1256 | < 0.001 | 72,575 |
| The new courier delivers faster in Greece | Mann-Whitney U (one-sided; delivery days are skewed, so no t-test) | median 5 -> 4 days, mean 5.36 -> 3.89 | U = 8.47e+08 | < 0.001 | 70,226 |
| Average order value fell in 2025 (bulk orders excluded) | Welch t-test + bootstrap 95% CI (1,000 resamples) | EUR 168.25 -> 154.54 (diff -13.70, 95% CI -15.23 to -12.11) | t = -17.0 | < 0.001 | 136,922 |
| Cash-on-delivery orders are cancelled more often | Two-proportion z-test | 4.5% other methods vs 10.2% COD | z = 35.0 | < 0.001 | 144,790 |

Notes:

- The data covers the whole customer base, not a sample, so very small p-values are expected for
  large effects. The confidence intervals and effect sizes matter more than the p-values.
- Test 2 is the important control: two things changed in 2025, so the loyalty effect is re-tested
  among customers whose first delivery was on time, which removes the courier change from the comparison.
- These are observational comparisons, not an experiment: a before/after difference supports, but
  does not prove, that the programme caused it. A holdout group would.
